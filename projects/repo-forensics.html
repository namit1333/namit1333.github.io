Repo Forensics

It reads a software project’s diary and points to where the trouble is most likely to come from.

The problem, in plain words

Every software project keeps a perfect diary. Every change ever made is recorded: what was touched, when, and by whom. Almost nobody reads it.

Yet that diary answers the questions teams ask constantly. Which files keep breaking? Which parts does only one person understand? What always has to be changed together? Today those questions get answered by gut feeling and hallway conversations. They can be answered by evidence.

What it looks at

  1. The hotspots.Files that are still changing. A file that churned three years ago isn’t a problem; a file that keeps changing this month is where maintenance time actually goes.
  2. The one-person risks.Places where almost all the knowledge sits with a single person. If they’re on holiday — or gone — work stops. In the trade this is called the “bus factor”: how many people would have to disappear before the project stalls.
  3. The secret couples.Pairs of files that always change together. Usually it means one thing has been split in two, and they’ll keep causing double work until someone reunites them.

In everyday terms

It’s like reading a building’s maintenance records before you buy it. The leaks leave a pattern — if you know how to read it.

Why it matters

Maintenance is where software time quietly goes. Knowing which files are hotspots, which knowledge is fragile, and which pieces are secretly joined helps a team spend its attention where it matters — before something breaks, not after.

Who it’s for: team leads planning work, people taking over a project they didn’t write, and anyone who’s ever asked “where should we be careful?”

What you get

Two reports of the same findings: one written for people — clean enough to read in a meeting — and one written for other tools to consume. The maths behind it is deliberately simple, and every number is checked against results worked out by hand.

Good to know

  • It only reads history — it never changes the project.
  • Recent activity counts more than old activity, on purpose.
  • It measures how much was changed, not just how often a file was touched.
